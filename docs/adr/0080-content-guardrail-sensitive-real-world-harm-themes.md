# ADR-0080: Generation guardrail against real-world-sensitive harm themes (sexual assault, trafficking, predation on young women)

- **Status:** Accepted — both child (Child27) and parent (Parent53) imported to Make.com 2026-08-13; live-confirm against a real production generation still pending for both
- **Date:** 2026-08-13
- **Related:** `docs/generation-guardrails.md` (G1–G11 canonical rule list, G12 entry), ADR-0042 (content-quality detectors + G7–G10)

## Correction (same day, before this ADR was closed out)

The Decision section below originally concluded the parent's master_context generators were "not a real vector for this specific defect" and deliberately scoped G12 to the child scenario only, based on checking the master_context **schema** (no dedicated multi-paragraph prose field). That was the wrong thing to check. Pulling the actual `master_context` row for the Avery Blackwood package showed the seed already there, generated by the Parent: `howVictimWrongedThem`, `threatsOrPressures`, and `specificMotive` (Part 1 and Part 2 outputs) all contained a version of *"Natasha also had dirt on how Avery mentors young models — potentially exploitative dynamics or knowledge of misconduct Avery witnessed or enabled."* The Child didn't originate this theme; it inherited a seed the Parent had already planted and elaborated it into full prose. Corrected by building the guardrail into the Parent's Part 1/Part 2 prompts as well (both style routes) — see the updated Decision and Key files below. Left as a visible correction rather than silently editing the original text, since the wrong reasoning is itself a useful data point (checking a prompt's *schema* for a content risk is not the same as checking what it actually generates).

## Context

Customer Heather Ainsworth (`heather.a@placearc.com`, package "The Host Herself", conversation `ac2c3610-7078-477a-9375-18e1a282a034`) emailed asking us to change her character Avery Blackwood, whose backstory was: former supermodel turned talent recruiter who witnessed a photographer preying on young models, stayed silent about it for years, and is being blackmailed over that silence by the victim. Heather's concern was specifically the combination of "exposing girls" and the current political climate — a fair read, since the secret centers on an adult enabling predatory behavior toward young women and is delivered as a paid party-game confession one of her guests may read aloud.

Investigation before the fix:
- The storyline was fully self-contained to Avery's own character sheet — no other character, the host guide, detective script, evidence cards, or game overview referenced it. Confirmed by grepping every text field in the package for "Avery" and "model".
- A DB-wide search for the same thematic combination (modeling/predator/silence-about-misconduct-toward-young-women) across every generated package found no other match. This is not a repeating pattern in delivered content — it's a one-off instance of the character-generation prompt landing on a real-world-sensitive trope combination.
- The child scenario's `content_coherence_rules` (the block that governs every per-character field — see `docs/generation-guardrails.md` G1–G9) has no exclusion list for this class of theme at all. Grepped the latest live-family blueprint (`MM Live - Child (Unified)26-DropSelfCheckTruncationFix.blueprint.json`) and the parent scenario for "trafficking", "exploitation", "predator", "underage", "minor" — none present. Whatever informal "no trafficking" understanding existed was never written into a prompt or this doc; there is nothing to point to as prior art.

Murder-mystery secrets are supposed to be dark — affairs, fraud, blackmail, and buried scandal are the genre's entire mechanism, and the business intentionally writes spicy adult content. The gap here isn't "secrets are too dark" in general; it's a specific combination — real-world-sensitive harm to minors or young women (sexual assault, trafficking, exploitation, predation) used as a character's secret or motive — that reads badly regardless of how well-written it is, because it invokes real ongoing news stories about actual victims rather than genre-appropriate fictional crime.

## Decision

Add a new guardrail (**G12**) to `docs/generation-guardrails.md`'s canonical rule list, to be inserted into the child scenario's `content_coherence_rules` block (all character-based and detective-style routes) and the parent's master_context generators (both style routes), following the same "prompt text ready, blueprint build is a separate step" convention already used for G7–G10 and the G11 recipe.

**Rule scope:** character secrets, backstories, motives, and rumors must not center on sexual assault, human trafficking, or the sexual exploitation/predation of minors or young women — whether the character is the perpetrator, an enabler, or someone who witnessed and stayed silent. This is a narrower carve-out than "no dark content generally" — fraud, violence, affairs, addiction, and other adult themes standard to the genre remain unrestricted.

> Child `content_coherence_rules` (both style routes), mirrored in `regenerate-child-content`:
> *"REAL-WORLD-SENSITIVE HARM THEMES: Do not write any character's secret, backstory, motive, or rumor around sexual assault, human trafficking, or the sexual exploitation/predation of minors or young women — whether this character is the perpetrator, an enabler, or someone who witnessed such harm and stayed silent about it. This applies regardless of the character's role (suspect, murderer, accomplice, victim). Other dark or adult themes (fraud, violence, addiction, affairs, blackmail over non-sexual misconduct) remain in scope and do not need to be softened."*
>
> Parent master_context Part 1 (VICTIM PROFILE section) and Part 2 (MOTIVE DISTRIBUTION section), both style routes — four insertion points total, phrased for the master_context's compact fields rather than the child's prose fields:
> *"Content boundary: Do not write any character's grievance, threat, or wrong here around sexual assault, human trafficking, or the sexual exploitation/predation of minors or young women — whether as perpetrator, enabler, or silent witness. This applies to the victim's conduct and to any character's backstory. Other dark themes (fraud, violence, addiction, affairs, non-sexual blackmail) remain fully in scope."*
> No edge-function mirror needed for the parent side: `regenerate-parent-content` consumes an existing `master_context` to rebuild the four host-facing documents (game_overview, materials, detective_script, evidence_cards) — it never regenerates `master_context` itself, so there is no standalone code path this rule would apply to outside the live Make.com blueprint.

**Fix for the specific customer:** Avery Blackwood's description, background, secret, introduction, one relationships bullet, one accusations bullet, both round-2 branches, both round-3 branches, and the guilty confession were rewritten to a Wall Street financial-fraud storyline (witnessed and stayed silent about a colleague's fraud, now runs a recruiting firm) — same narrative shape and puzzle mechanics (blackmail motive, toxicology red herring via pharma/biotech clients), same relationships to other suspects, none of the removed content. Applied directly to `mystery_characters` (id `3e0f551a-86e5-4716-9793-c0e93678847f`) since the package is already paid and delivered; there is no regeneration path that wouldn't risk collateral changes to unrelated fields.

## Rationale

- The DB sweep found this is not a live, repeating defect — it's a single instance — so there's no urgent backlog to sweep, but the fact that nothing prevents it from recurring is a real gap given the generator writes character secrets somewhat freely from open-ended chat context (a "girls' weekend" or similar premise is exactly the kind of setup that could produce this combination again).
- Scoping the rule narrowly (sexual assault / trafficking / predation on minors or young women) rather than broadly ("no dark themes") preserves the genre's actual product — customers buy these mysteries for blackmail, affairs, and buried scandal, and a broad content softening would hurt the product for no reason connected to this incident.
- Following the existing G7–G10/G11 convention (prompt text specified here, blueprint import is a separate manual step, ledger tracks live-vs-built) rather than editing the Make.com blueprint directly, because a blind edit to a multi-hundred-KB blueprint JSON has caused real incidents before (see the blueprint-versioning hazard in memory) and this rule needs review before it ships to a live, revenue-generating scenario.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Rely on Claude's own model-level safety training instead of an explicit prompt rule.** Rejected: the Avery Blackwood content is proof the model-level defaults alone don't reliably keep this combination out — the storyline was coherent, well-written, and not something a general safety filter would flag, because nothing in it is actually disallowed content; it's genre-appropriate-looking prose that happens to land on a sensitive real-world combination. An explicit business rule is needed because this is a taste/brand-risk judgment, not a safety-classifier judgment.
- **Broaden to "avoid all real-world political/news-adjacent sensitivity."** Rejected as too vague to be an enforceable prompt rule — "political climate" is exactly the phrase the customer used, but a prompt instruction needs a concrete, checkable boundary. Sexual assault/trafficking/predation-on-young-women is the specific class this incident actually surfaced; broadening further can be revisited if another distinct pattern shows up.
- **Sweep and pre-emptively rewrite any other packages that might contain adjacent-but-not-identical themes (e.g. non-sexual predatory business practices, generic "dark industry" secrets).** Rejected for now: the DB search found no other match for this specific combination, and a broader keyword sweep for adjacent-but-different themes (fraud, generic "predatory," non-sexual exploitation) returned only false positives on manual spot-check. Revisit if a second real instance surfaces.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** with both the child and parent blueprints carrying G12, no future generated package should be able to land on this specific trope combination at either the seed (Parent motive/threat fields) or the elaboration (Child prose) stage.
- **Shipped 2026-08-13:** Child27 built and imported to Make.com by Jonathan same day (live-confirm against a real production generation still pending). `regenerate-child-content` v3→v5 deployed and confirmed live.
- **Shipped 2026-08-13:** Parent53 (`temp-files/MM Live - Parent53 (Real-World Harm Themes Guardrail).blueprint.json`) — validated (JSON valid, module count unchanged at 164, all 4 insertions spot-checked in context), imported to Make.com same day (Jonathan). Live-confirm against a real production generation pending for both Child27 and Parent53.
- **Not addressed:** no sweep for other, differently-shaped real-world-sensitive combinations beyond the one this incident surfaced — see Alternatives Considered.

Since the DB-wide character-field sweep found no other match, and the Child is the layer that turns a seed into player-facing prose, this is lower priority — but if a second incident surfaces, check `master_context` text directly rather than only the rendered character fields, per the lesson in the Correction above.

## Discussion

The customer's framing ("the political climate of the world right now") is doing real work here — this isn't a generic content-quality bug like G1–G11, which fix internal-consistency defects (timing, self-directed questions, chain-of-thought leaks). This is a brand/reputational-risk judgment about what kind of dark content is acceptable for the product to generate, even when it's internally coherent and well-written. That's a business-taste call, not a bug fix. The narrow scope (sexual assault/trafficking/predation on minors or young women specifically, not dark themes generally) reflects a deliberate choice to fix the actual incident rather than use it as a pretext to sand down the product's edge more broadly.

The same-day correction (see above) is its own small case of a pattern this codebase has been bitten by repeatedly under other names — trusting a plan/schema/template as a proxy for what actually got generated, instead of checking the real artifact. `project_stale_snapshot_never_recaptures` and `project_concept_snapshot_header_rename` are two prior instances of the same shape at the data layer; this is the same mistake one layer up, at the prompt-design layer: "the master_context schema has no prose field for this" was treated as equivalent to "the master_context has never produced this," when the actual generated `master_context` for the one real incident on file said otherwise. The fix pattern is the same each time: read the actual row, not the shape it's supposed to have.
