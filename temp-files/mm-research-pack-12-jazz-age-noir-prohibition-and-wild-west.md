### Jazz Age vs. Roaring Twenties: Key Differentiation

While often used interchangeably, the Jazz Age and Roaring Twenties themes offer distinct narrative possibilities for murder mystery designers:

**Jazz Age emphasis:** The cultural revolution aspect — the collision between Black musical innovation and white mainstream culture, the speakeasy as a democratic social space, the musicians as celebrities and outsiders simultaneously. Jazz Age murder mysteries can explore questions of appropriation, racial tension, and the price of fame in ways that Gatsby-focused scenarios may not.

**Roaring Twenties emphasis:** The economic and social dynamics — new money vs. old money, the gap between public respectability and private vice, the emergence of consumer culture and celebrity. Roaring Twenties scenarios tend toward glamorous mansion settings and wealthy suspects.

### The Noir Detective Aesthetic: Setting Design Notes

Film noir's visual language is immediately recognizable even to audiences with no specific film history knowledge — because it has been absorbed into mainstream popular culture through decades of homage, parody, and reference. This makes noir detective themes paradoxically both accessible and sophisticated: guests don't need to know Dashiell Hammett to immediately understand the aesthetic and embrace the character types.

**Visual elements that signal "noir" to contemporary audiences:**
- Venetian blind shadow patterns
- Single-source dramatic lighting
- Cigarette smoke (real or theatrical fog)
- Rain-slicked surfaces and dark color palettes
- Trenchcoats, fedoras, high heels
- Film posters, wanted posters, newspaper headlines as décor
- Black-and-white photography and monochrome styling

**Character archetypes that guests immediately inhabit:**
- The Hard-Boiled Detective (skeptical, world-weary, ultimately principled)
- The Femme Fatale (dangerous, sophisticated, motivations uncertain)
- The Corrupt Official (respectable surface, rotten interior)
- The Helpless Client (appears innocent, may not be)
- The Mob Boss (power, menace, code of honor)
- The Nightclub Singer (knows everyone's secrets)

**Writing tone for blog posts targeting this theme:** Adopt a slightly hard-boiled narrative voice in the blog copy itself — it demonstrates thematic authenticity and creates more engaging, distinctive content than generic party planning prose.

### Noir and the Murder Mystery Genre: Historical Connection

The noir detective tradition and the murder mystery party tradition share a single literary ancestor: the "Golden Age" detective fiction of the 1920s-1940s. Agatha Christie, Raymond Chandler, Dashiell Hammett — these writers invented both the puzzles that power contemporary murder mystery games AND the atmospheric conventions that define the noir aesthetic. Understanding this connection helps position mysterymaker.party as participating in a continuous literary tradition, not just selling party games.

### The Psychology of Speakeasy Settings
